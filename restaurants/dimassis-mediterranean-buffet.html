Dimassi's Mediterranean Buffet

All-You-Can-Eat Halal Mediterranean — South Austin

33+ GF Items Labeled Halal Shared Kitchen

✅ Good for Gluten Sensitivity — ⚠️ Use Caution with Celiac

Dimassi's labels 33+ of their 60+ buffet dishes as gluten free, making it easy to navigate. The Mediterranean menu is naturally GF-friendly — hummus, falafel, kabobs, rice, grilled vegetables. The kitchen is shared, so it's not a safe choice for severe celiac, but it's one of the more GF-accommodating buffets in Austin for people with gluten sensitivity.

What Makes Dimassi's GF-Friendly

Mediterranean cuisine is naturally well-suited to gluten-free eating — lots of grilled proteins, legumes, rice, and vegetable dishes that don't require wheat. Dimassi's leans into this with clear labeling: out of 60+ rotating buffet dishes, more than 33 are marked gluten free on the buffet line.

The labeling makes it practical. Instead of guessing or interrogating staff at every dish, you can move through the buffet and identify safe items at a glance. For people managing gluten sensitivity in a group setting — work lunches, family dinners, gatherings with mixed dietary needs — Dimassi's is one of the more straightforward options in Austin.

What's Typically GF at Dimassi's

The buffet rotates, but these items are consistently present and GF-labeled:

  • Hummus — the classic, and reliably GF
  • Baba ghanoush — roasted eggplant dip, GF
  • Falafel — check the current label, some preparations use breadcrumbs
  • Chicken kabobs and beef kabobs — grilled, GF
  • Grilled vegetables — zucchini, eggplant, peppers
  • Rice dishes — most are GF; one rice dish contains fine pasta (vermicelli), so check the label
  • Lentil soup — typically GF
  • Various salads — most are GF; tabbouleh is NOT (bulgur wheat)
  • Roasted chicken — typically GF

What to Avoid

⚠️ Items to Skip

Even with good GF labeling, a few items are easy to mistake for safe:

  • Tabbouleh — contains bulgur wheat, NOT GF despite looking like a salad
  • Rice with vermicelli — one of the rice dishes has fine pasta mixed in; check the label carefully
  • Pita bread — wheat, obviously skip
  • Baklava — phyllo dough, contains wheat
  • Pizza — Dimassi's includes a pizza section at some locations; wheat crust

Celiac Disease Considerations

Dimassi's is a shared kitchen buffet environment. Serving utensils are used across multiple dishes, the kitchen prepares wheat-containing items alongside GF ones, and the buffet format itself creates cross-contamination opportunities that are difficult to control.

The GF labeling is genuinely helpful, but it indicates ingredient-level GF status — not preparation-level safety for celiac disease. If you have severe celiac, the honest answer is that a buffet environment is a difficult setting regardless of how many items are labeled.

For people with gluten sensitivity who can tolerate trace exposure, Dimassi's is a solid option with a wide variety of naturally GF dishes. For celiac, approach with caution and focus on items least likely to have cross-contact.

The Overall Experience

Beyond the GF considerations, Dimassi's is genuinely a good meal. The buffet is extensive, the food is fresh, and the price point for all-you-can-eat is reasonable. It's a halal restaurant with a wide range of Mediterranean staples — the kind of place that works well for groups with varied dietary needs because there's something for everyone.

The South Austin location on E Stassney is easy to get to from I-35. Note that the former Research Boulevard location has closed — the Stassney location is the only Austin outpost currently open.
