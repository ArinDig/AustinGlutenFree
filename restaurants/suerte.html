The Short Answer

Yes — and Suerte is one of the better non-dedicated options in Austin for celiac diners. The restaurant is built around masa: they nixtamalize heirloom corn in-house, so the tortillas and chips are corn rather than flour. There's a gluten-free menu, a dedicated fryer, and staff who ask about your specific level of cross-contact sensitivity rather than giving a generic answer. It is still a shared kitchen, so it isn't risk-free — but the fundamentals are unusually strong.

About Suerte

Suerte is an award-winning East Austin restaurant driven by a passion for masa — the corn dough at the heart of Mexican cooking. Led by Chef Fermín Núñez, it has earned a place in the MICHELIN Guide and national recognition for treating corn as the centerpiece rather than an afterthought.

That focus is the whole reason this page exists. Most Mexican restaurants buy tortillas. Suerte sources heirloom corn varieties and nixtamalizes them on site — the traditional process of cooking and steeping corn in an alkaline solution before grinding it into masa. It's a labor-intensive choice made for flavor, not for dietary accommodation.

But the side effect matters enormously if you can't eat wheat: when a kitchen's entire identity is built on corn masa, the gluten-free version of the meal isn't a downgraded substitute. It's the actual dish everyone else is ordering.

Gluten-Free Dining at Suerte

What Suerte does well, specifically:

  • Gluten-Free Menu: A dedicated GF menu is available — ask when you're seated
  • All Corn Tortillas: Tortillas are made in-house from heirloom corn they nixtamalize themselves — no flour tortillas to confuse the order
  • Corn Tortilla Chips: Chips are corn as well
  • Dedicated Fryer: This is the standout. A separate fryer removes the single most common source of cross-contact at Mexican restaurants
  • Staff Ask About Your Sensitivity Level: Rather than a blanket "we can do gluten-free," servers reportedly ask how sensitive you are and adjust handling accordingly — a meaningful sign of real training
  • Gluten-Free Cocktails: The bar program has GF-friendly options too

Cross-Contamination Note: Suerte is not a dedicated gluten-free facility, and shared prep surfaces and utensils remain a possibility. The dedicated fryer and all-corn tortilla program remove two of the largest risks, but if you are highly sensitive, tell your server explicitly and ask about utensil and surface handling for your specific dishes. If you need a fully dedicated kitchen, ATX Cocina is 100% gluten-free and also nixtamalizes its own heirloom corn.

Why the Corn Matters for Celiac Diners

Mexican food is often assumed to be an easy gluten-free cuisine, but the reality in Austin is more complicated. Tex-Mex kitchens typically run flour and corn tortillas side by side, share fryers between tortilla chips and breaded items, and use flour as a thickener in sauces, queso, and gravies.

Suerte inverts that. Because the kitchen is organized around house-made corn masa, the default preparation is already wheat-free rather than a modification requested off-script. Combined with the dedicated fryer, that structural difference is worth more than a restaurant simply offering to "make something gluten-free" on request.

Reservations

Suerte takes reservations for parties of up to 8 through OpenTable. Parties of 9 or more should contact the restaurant directly about large-party dining.

Tip for gluten-free diners: Add a note to your reservation mentioning celiac or gluten sensitivity. It gives the kitchen advance warning and tends to produce a smoother, better-informed conversation at the table than raising it cold when you sit down.
