About Casa de Luz

Casa de Luz is a plant-based community kitchen and gathering space on Toomey Road near Zilker Park. It serves macrobiotic, whole-food meals, cooks without processed oils (fats come from whole seeds and nuts), and doesn't post a fixed menu: dishes change daily based on fresh, organic ingredients.

The main plate always includes a grain, a legume, dark leafy greens with a seed or nut sauce, mixed vegetables and fermented pickled vegetables, with soup and salad included. Breakfast, from 7 AM until it runs out, adds handmade tortillas and porridge with granola. It's open every day from 7 AM to 8:30 PM, with pickup ordering online.

Is Casa de Luz Gluten-Free?

Many guides list Casa de Luz as fully gluten-free, and diners often describe it that way. The restaurant itself doesn't say so on its website. It says only that its cooks welcome dietary restrictions if you let them know.

Two things matter for celiac diners:

  • The grains vary. Every main plate includes “pre-soaked whole grains,” and the specific grain changes. Most macrobiotic kitchens lean on rice and millet, but barley, wheat berries and farro are also common in that style of cooking.
  • Oats. Breakfast porridge and granola use oats. Unless they're certified gluten-free, regular oats are often cross-contaminated with wheat, and some celiacs react to oats regardless.

If you have celiac disease, ask before ordering: which grain is in today's plate, whether the oats are certified gluten-free, and whether any wheat, barley or rye is used in the kitchen.

If you want a kitchen that publishes a 100% gluten-free policy, Picnik on Barton Springs is close by.
